# Persona 1 — The Outfit Seeker

> **⚠️ COMPOSITE. NOT A REAL INTERVIEW.** Synthesized from publicly available Zalando reviews and Zalando Design's own published user research. Name and demographics are constructed; behaviors and frustrations are drawn from cited verbatim quotes (`raw-quotes.csv`) and the case study's eight identified problems. **This persona's outfit/occasion JTBD is HYPOTHESIS-STAGE in the current corpus** (no quote in `raw-quotes.csv` explicitly mentions occasion-driven shopping). See `methodology-disclosure.md` for source-coverage audit.

---

## Composite sketch

- **Name (composite):** Léa
- **Where (composite):** Antwerp / Brussels / Paris — major Western European city
- **Trigger to visit Zalando:** an upcoming occasion — wedding, interview, holiday, work event — with a specific date and dress code
- **Mental model when shopping:** "I need a head-to-toe outfit by [date]. I have one shot to get it right."

## JTBD statement

> **When** I have an occasion coming up and need a complete outfit by a specific date, **I want to** assemble a coordinated head-to-toe look in one session without bouncing across the catalog, **so that** I can order, try, return what doesn't fit, and have the final outfit ready before the event.

## Top 3 frustrations (observation + hypothesis flagged)

1. **Fulfillment timing risk against a date** — *observation*. Multiple long-term customers report that delivery promises don't hold up under date pressure:  
   > "Delivery times are not at all what is promised when you are ordering. It would be fairer not to promise delivery in 1-3 days when its actually more than one week." — Anonymous, reviews.io (quote #18)
   > "I have been ordering from zalando for last 3 years. Since 2023 mid, the orders are returned before delivering to me. Reason for the return: They could not locate the delivery address." — Anonymous, reviews.io (quote #14)
   > "Orders Sent payment nothing was delivered, they said it had been absolutely terrible customer service I have waited over 3 months." — Anonymous, reviews.io (quote #16)

2. **No outfit-level construct that survives the multi-tab browsing** — *hypothesis*. Outfit Seekers must mentally hold a "where I am in the outfit" state across many PDPs. No quote in the corpus directly names this — but the eight identified problems list this gap as core (Problem #2: "Complete the Look" misnamed; Problem #7: naming inconsistency between "Complete the Look" entry and "Get the Look" destination).

3. **Wrong-item-on-arrival kills the timeline** — *observation*. When the outfit needs to land by Saturday, a wrong-size item received Friday means the whole outfit fails:  
   > "I have made few purchases from them 2 weeks ago, everything was good until received the goods, 3 items were good but on was not the size I've ordered." — Khaled H, reviews.io (quote #10)
   > "Nella scatola inviata non erano presenti 2 articoli (PAGATI) e nemmeno la documentazione per effettuare il reso." (Translation: 2 items missing from the package, no return documentation) — Ivan Plachesi, reviews.io (quote #12)
   > "I have been buying from Zalando for many years and never had any real issues other than being sent the wrong sizes from time to time… Until recently." — Helen, reviews.io (quote #11)

## Top 3 needs

1. **A coherent, occasion-tagged outfit primitive** that holds together across PDPs (hypothesis-stage in the corpus; ties to the case study's Look Detail Page redesign on surface #2).
2. **Reliable delivery against a stated deadline** — sometimes more important than catalog breadth.
3. **A frictionless return path** for the wrong-size / wrong-item that doesn't blow up the rest of the outfit.

## Behavior patterns

- Visits Zalando with a deadline in mind, not for leisure browsing.
- Tolerates returns as a buy-multiple-keep-one strategy — but only if the return process is predictable.
- Mentions Amazon as a comparison when fulfillment falters:  
  > "Please, this company has liars and scammers in customer service. Save nerves, time and money and buy at amazon." — Anonymous, reviews.io (quote #15) — *cross-cuts to Bargain Hunter; included here because the Amazon-switch threat appears whenever delivery promise breaks down, which is Outfit Seeker's hot button*

## What Zalando does well for them (when it works)

- Multi-brand catalog gives access to multiple price points and styles in one place:  
  > "I love ordering from Zalando. They have a huge variety of items from different brands, the shipment is most of the time fast and if you need assistance with anything, the customer service is very prompt to help." — Fernanda, reviews.io (quote #8)
  > "I love Zalando, I reside in Spain. Very good quality products, fair prizes and they always deliver what you ordered and always on time." — Anonymous, reviews.io (quote #9)

## What fails them

- Outfit-completion widgets that surface monotone categories (Problem #4 in case study) when they need head-to-toe.
- Delivery promises that don't survive against an event deadline (corpus-strong).
- Wrong items on arrival when there's no slack to re-order (corpus-strong).

## Voice quote

If Léa wrote a one-line review of her last outfit-seeking Zalando trip, it would mash up two real quotes in tone:

> "I love that Zalando has so many brands in one place — but when you're trying to get an outfit for a specific date, *'delivered in 1-3 days'* turning into 'more than one week' breaks the whole plan."  
> (composition of corpus quotes #8 and #18 — illustrative of voice, not a real single quote; this is the only constructed quote in the persona file and is explicitly labelled as such)

## Connection to redesign

- Surface #1 (PDP Complete the Look widget) — needs cross-category enforcement so the Outfit Seeker isn't shown 5 more jeans.
- Surface #2 (Look Detail Page) — needs the outfit-level save and a bundle "add all" action.
- Surface #3 (Discovery between creators) — secondary for this persona; they have a specific date, not an inspiration goal.
