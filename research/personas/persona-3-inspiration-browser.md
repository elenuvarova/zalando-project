# Persona 3 — The Inspiration Browser

> **⚠️ COMPOSITE. NOT A REAL INTERVIEW.** Synthesized from publicly available Zalando reviews and Zalando Design's own published user research. Name and demographics are constructed; behaviors and frustrations are drawn from cited verbatim quotes (`raw-quotes.csv`). **This persona has the strongest corpus evidence of the five** — the two Zalando-Design-published user research quotes (#20, #21) directly speak to this archetype. See `methodology-disclosure.md`.

---

## Composite sketch

- **Name (composite):** Anouk
- **Where (composite):** Amsterdam / Berlin / Antwerp — a designer-aware, fashion-curious customer
- **Trigger to visit Zalando:** no specific intent — a lunch break, an evening, a weekend morning. "Let me see what's new."
- **Mental model when shopping:** "Show me something I didn't know I wanted. I'll save it if it catches me, buy it if I love it."

## JTBD statement

> **When** I have no specific shopping intent but want to engage with fashion, **I want to** browse a feed that surfaces things I'd genuinely like — visually, by mood, by style — without making me articulate what I'm looking for, **so that** I find things I didn't know existed and either save them for later or buy on impulse.

## Top 3 frustrations

1. **Choice overload — can't translate "I want to browse" into a useful path** — *observation, Zalando's own published research*:  
   > "I feel overwhelmed by choice while exploring Zalando." — Research participant, Zalando Design Medium (quote #20)

2. **The vocabulary gap — knows what they like when they see it, can't search for it** — *observation, Zalando's own published research*:  
   > "Sometimes I don't know the word for what I am looking for and then it is perfect to just see it and be able to click on it." — User feedback, Zalando Design Medium (quote #21)

3. **Updates that break learned behavior — when the browsing experience changes, exploration regresses** — *observation*:  
   > "Seit dem Update sind, meiner Meinung nach, viele Funktionen so viel komplizierter geworden." (Translation: "Since the update, in my opinion, many functions have become so much more complicated.") — Marie LB, App Store DE (quote #22)
   > "I used to be an avid Zalando fan, with multiple purchase per month. Lately orders started to go lost…" — L, reviews.io (quote #2) — *cross-cuts to fulfillment but tone matches: long-time browser whose habit got disrupted*

## Top 3 needs

1. **Visual entry points** that don't require articulating what you want — confirmed by Zalando's own redesign research (quotes #20, #21 were the basis for Zalando's visual-filter redesign per the Medium article).
2. **Style-coherent recommendations** that feel curated, not algorithmic-random. (Hypothesis-stage as user voice; observation-stage as case-study premise, Problem #5: "Zero style coherence — light-blue wide-leg denim paired with radically different vibes.")
3. **A persistent way to save what catches the eye** — at outfit level, not just item level. (Hypothesis as user voice; observation as case-study premise — Pinterest's board pattern is the canonical solve.)

## Behavior patterns

- Opens Zalando without a goal — homepage, "new in", creator outfits, editorial.
- Multi-tab opens: keeps 5-10 items in tabs across a session, may or may not return.
- High variety pull — multi-brand, multi-category browsing in one session:  
  > "They have a huge variety of items from different brands" — Fernanda, reviews.io (quote #8)
- Susceptible to friction. The Inspiration Browser is the persona most likely to abandon when the surface feels random or repetitive — they had no commitment to begin with.

## What Zalando does well for them (when it works)

- Multi-brand catalog gives genuine variety, which is the Inspiration Browser's lifeblood (quote #8).
- Visual filtering — added in the redesign Zalando Design itself documented — directly addresses quote #21's articulation gap.

## What fails them

- The "Better together" widget showing random tops without coherence (Problem #3) — disengages the Inspiration Browser immediately. Without coherence, every fresh visit becomes the same noise.
- Algorithmic grouping that surfaces similar items rather than stylistic affinity (Problem #6) — produces sameness, which is fatal for inspiration browsing.
- "Complete the Look" widget that returns nothing of interest because tagging is off (Problem #1) — Inspiration Browser sees creator-photo-soup, not outfit logic.

## Voice quote (verbatim)

> "Sometimes I don't know the word for what I am looking for and then it is perfect to just see it and be able to click on it."  
> — User feedback, Zalando Design Medium (quote #21) — captures the visual-first, vocabulary-poor browsing instinct exactly

## Connection to redesign

- Surface #1 (PDP widget) — coherence + rationale (the "why these go together" layer from competitive scan's best practice #4) speaks directly to this persona's "show me curated, not random" need.
- Surface #2 (Look Detail Page) — the Look as a coherent unit with explanation IS the Inspiration Browser's payoff.
- Surface #3 (Discovery between creators by style cluster) — **this persona is the primary target of the discovery navigation redesign**. The force-directed style-cluster graph (Week 3 of the case study) is justified by this persona's "browse without articulating" JTBD.
