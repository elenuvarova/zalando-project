## How to read this matrix

- **Zalando's column is the baseline**; every other row should be read as "does this competitor solve, share, or worsen the named Zalando problem?"
- A row of mostly ✅ doesn't mean "we should copy this competitor wholesale" — luxury (Net-a-Porter, SSENSE) and closed-catalog (Stitch Fix, Uniqlo) models are not directly portable to Zalando's multi-brand marketplace at scale.
- The most transferable patterns cluster around dimensions **4 (rationale)**, **5 (bundle)**, **6 (save-as-outfit)**, and **7 (cross-category)** — see [`strategic-findings.md`](strategic-findings.md).
- 🔍 cells mark items where direct verification wasn't possible from public sources during this scan; treat as gaps in confidence, not as default-positive.

## Quick takeaways

- **No mainstream e-commerce competitor surfaces an explicit style-coherence rationale** ("we paired these because…") on the PDP. This is the clearest empty quadrant.
- **Bundle action ("add all to bag")** is almost universally weak across the matrix. ABOUT YOU and Stitch Fix are the exceptions; Stitch Fix only "wins" because the bundle is the product, not a UI affordance on a PDP.
- **Save-as-outfit** as a primary save granularity is rare outside Pinterest, Whering, and Stitch Fix — and those three define their products around the outfit unit, not the item unit. This is structurally why Zalando struggles here: the item is its commerce unit.
- **Tagging accuracy** is a moat for closed catalogs (Uniqlo, Stitch Fix, Mango, Farfetch, Net-a-Porter). Multi-brand marketplaces (Zalando, Lyst, ASOS, ABOUT YOU) inherit messier signal.
